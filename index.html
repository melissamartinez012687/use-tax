Missouri & Kansas Use Tax Calculator

Type a city, pick it, and the official use tax rate and codes fill in. Add the invoice number and amount to calculate use tax. Invoices are grouped into Missouri, Kansas (other), Overland Park (Y), and Lenexa (Z) and saved in this browser.

Paste invoices

One per line: invoice, city, amount. State and tax already paid are optional, e.g. INV-7, Joplin, MO, $1,250.00, 20.00. Y and Z work as the city for Overland Park and Lenexa. Add the word exempt at the end for a tax-exempt invoice, with an optional reason: INV-8, Garfield, AR, 11000, exempt out-of-state delivery. Commas, tabs, or semicolons all work.

How the rates work

This is an estimate, not tax advice. Rates change each quarter, and special districts (CIDs, TDDs, STAR bond areas) inside a city can change the rate at some addresses. Confirm with the Missouri DOR or the Kansas Department of Revenue before filing.

Missouri: rates are the Use Tax Rate column of the DOR Sales and Use Tax Rate Tables, October–December 2026 (updated 8/25/2026). Missouri use rates often differ from sales rates, and some places have no local use tax, so their rate is 4.225% (state only). The jurisdiction code is the DOR's: the first 5 digits are the city code, the middle 3 the county code, and the last 3 the district code. The DOR table lists only total rates, so the local portion is shown as total minus the 4.225% state rate. 1,644 Missouri places: every city/county combination plus every county's unincorporated area.

Kansas: Pub. KS-1700 has no separate use tax column. It states that "the Kansas retailers' sales tax and use tax rate is a combination of the state rate plus any local tax percentage levied by a county or a city", and Form CT-10U says to use the KS-1700 rate where the item is used, stored, or consumed. So the Kansas consumers' compensating use tax rate here is the KS-1700 combined rate (6.5% state + county + city), with the county, city, and special district portions from its columns. Where a Kansas row names no county, the county is filled only when the 2-letter code suffix is tied to exactly one county elsewhere in KS-1700; otherwise it is left blank. 750 Kansas places: cities plus county unincorporated areas, effective October 1, 2026.

The credit for tax paid to another state is limited to the use tax due on that invoice. Kansas allows it on CT-10U column 5. Missouri allows it by law, but Form 4340 has no separate credit line, so confirm how to report it with DOR (salesuse@dor.mo.gov).
